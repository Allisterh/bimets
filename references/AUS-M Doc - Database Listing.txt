Australian Macroeconomic (AUS-M) Model Database Documentation
Data Conventions
Unless otherwise stated all data have a quarterly frequency and are seasonally adjusted.
Constant price / volume data is untagged and has the same base year as the latest Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Australian National Accounts (presently 2009-10).
All indexes are equal to one in the base year with the base year being the same as that in the national accounts unless otherwise noted.
Mnemonic conventions
The model uses mnemonics rather than code to identify series. Each series has a unique mnemonic. The conventions followed are the same as those used by the Commonwealth Treasury in its forecasting spreadsheets and modelling. They originally derive from the 1970’s NIF (National Income Forecasting) model. The OECD’s Economic Outlook Database follows similar conventions derived from the OECD Interlink Model (which in turn were influenced by the NIF conventions). The following provides a guide to the intended meaning of the letters used as suffixes in individual mnemonics:
B – Borrowing: used in overseas net lending identities. (The model has a full set of stock flow identities - for the overseas net lending identities there is a corresponding V for each B.)  For example the current account balance is BCAZ, the Public Sector Borrowing Requirement is BPSBRZ. The corresponding stocks are VNFLZ (net foreign liabilities) and VDGZ (general government net debt) respectively. 
C – Consumption
I – Investment
E – Input output weighted demand item (variables that feed into the industry and import demand systems). EGi variables are input output weighted demand terms for industry value added at constant prices, while EMi variables are input output weighted demand terms for imports at constant prices.
G – Output, industry chain linked value added, aggregate expenditure items (e.g. GNE)
M – Imports
N – Number – used for labour force and population variables and hours worked, e.g. NLF labour force, NEi employment, NHi hours worked, NPi population.
P – Prices and national accounts deflators.
Q – Theoretical ratios (e.g. investment q ratios), dummy variables, structural change terms and time trends.
R – Rate or ratio – used for financial market interest rates (RI) and exchange rates (RE), depreciation rates (RD) and taxes (RT).
S – Stock-building / Inventory Investment
V – Value – used for wealth, borrowing and lending, for example the market value of private sector wealth is VMZ, the value of overseas net foreign liabilities VNFLZ.
W – World variable
X – Exports
As a rule nominal (current price) variables are tagged with a Z. For example the mnemonic/identifier for constant price and current price GDP (average measure) are GDPA and GDPAZ respectively. Often the current price variable is not separately identified but can be derived by multiplying the constant price variable by its respective deflator. For example current price dwelling investment IDWZ is not identified but can be derived as IDW*PIDW.

Ratio
For illustration. PIDW is a deflator for the construction component of established dwellings. Assuming construction productivity runs at around average levels, the deflator will rise at around the same rate as consumer prices.  PKDWE on the other hand includes a land component (which currently accounts for a around two thirds of the value of established dwellings on average). As land is in part a fixed resource its value should rise according to Hotellings principle at a rate approximating the expected rate of return on capital.  Hence the deflator should rise at a faster rate than consumer prices leading to a gradual increase in the ratio over time.
